# The delivery framework

Eight stages, six of them core. An offering declares only the ones it uses, but the ones it
declares always appear in this order, so a reader meets the same spine every time.

The stage list is a closed vocabulary. The titles below are exact, including the ampersands
and the en dash in `Readiness / Go–No-Go`.

---

## 1. Discover & Qualify — `T-90d → T-45d` (core)

Position the offering with the people who can fund and staff it, and qualify the engagement
**out** early if it does not fit.

This stage carries a gate that is easy to omit: the right tier, a real business problem, and
a named executive sponsor. An engagement that should have stopped here usually fails at
Prepare instead, after the cost has been incurred.

Good content: the pitch, the qualification questions, and the explicit disqualifiers.

## 2. Engage & Commit — `T-45d → T-30d` (core)

Turn interest into a joint commitment: named participants, locked dates, and target
outcomes written down and agreed by both sides.

The test of this stage is whether someone could later check whether the engagement
delivered what was promised. If the outcomes are not written down, it cannot.

Good content: the outcome-definition worksheet, the participant roster, the agreed dates.

## 3. Scope & Design — `T-30d → T-21d` (optional)

Tailor the agenda, challenges, and scenarios to the outcomes committed in stage 2.

Distinct from logistics, and genuinely irrelevant for fixed-curriculum training — mark it
`Not applicable` with that reason rather than deleting it, so the decision is visible.

Good content: challenge selection, archetype or scenario picks, the tailored agenda.

## 4. Prepare — `T-30d → T-7d` (core)

Two parallel tracks with different owners and different lead times:

- **Participant readiness** — comms, pre-reads, prerequisites, skilling.
- **Environment readiness** — golden tickets, subscriptions, quota and capacity, RBAC,
  model deployments, network access.

Quota and capacity requests for AI workloads are a `T-30d` item, not a `T-7d` item.
Collapsing both tracks into one undifferentiated block is what makes them get started late.

Good content: the joining instructions, the environment readiness checklist, who owns each.

## 5. Readiness / Go–No-Go — `T-7d → T-3d` (optional, recommended)

A dry run on the environment participants will **actually** use, followed by an explicit
go/no-go decision while there is still time to fix what it finds.

Most delivery failures are catchable here. Skipping it is a choice worth recording.

Good content: the dry-run script, the go/no-go criteria, the fallback plan.

## 6. Execute — `D0 → D+n` (core)

Deliver the engagement and capture evidence of progress as it happens, rather than
reconstructing it afterwards.

Good content: the run-of-show, facilitator notes, the daily rhythm, how evidence is captured.

## 7. Wrap & Close-out — `D0 → T+7d` (core)

The half of "wrap-up" with a hard deadline. Outcomes and demos captured **while people are
still in the room**, feedback collected, and the environment deprovisioned.

Deprovisioning is a cost and security obligation. An offering that leaves it implicit leaves
subscriptions running and access granted.

Good content: the demo capture format, the survey, the deprovisioning checklist.

## 8. Follow-up & Value realization — `T+7d → T+90d` (core)

The other half, on a completely different clock: commitments tracked, next steps progressed,
consumption or opportunity movement recorded — plus the internal retrospective that feeds
fixes back into this catalog.

Without the retro loop the offerings never mature, which is why it is core rather than
optional.

Good content: the commitment tracker, the 30/60/90 checkpoints, the retro template.

---

## What is deliberately not a stage

Roles and RACI, risks, compliance and legal (NDA, data handling, credit terms), cost and
funding model, accessibility and language. These are cross-cutting: they belong in the
offering's front matter or inside the individual stages, not as another box on the timeline.
